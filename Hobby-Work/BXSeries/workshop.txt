title=Ibishu BX-Series
description=[i]'...Your typical cheap sports car. In stock trim it's not much of a performer, but with the right modifications it can be the ultimate weapon of choice...'[/i]
description=- Gabester
description=
description=Originating from the videogame BeamNG.Drive, the BX-Series was Ibishu's answer to a flagship offering from their lineup. Unlike many other automakers however, where they would try to make something exotic, rare, or just plain over-the-top, Ibishu instead stuck to what they knew best. It was still affordable, reliable, and ubiquitous. There was only one new requirement added on: to make it 'fun'.
description=
description=Given the extreme success of the Ibishu Covet's performance lineup, the BX-Series was heavily focused in that market to offer a proper, purpose-built sports car. By taking heavy inspiration from British roadsters and American muscle cars from the late 60's and early 70's (along with some classic Ibishu quirks), it resulted in a remarkably nimble, front engine, rear wheel drive platform of either a liftback or coupe design that contained quite a lot of spunk for its size. 
description=
description=It released globally in 1990, offering two different engine models depending on its region. For Japan domestically, it was known as the 200BX, featuring a 2.0L I4 across its local lineups either as an economy car, or as an executive offering from Ibishu's sister brand; 'Diana'. The 240BX however, was intended for international markets, primarily North America with its larger 2.4L equivalent. This gave it more power than its Japanese cousins, and quickly conquered, if not propelled the relatively niche market of affordable sports cars into the mainstream for all to enjoy.
description=
description=[h1]Offered Models:[/h1]
description=
description=[b]DX:[/b] The entry-level trim. However, unlike the base level of Ibishu's other models, the DX level was offered with no definite compromises to the driving experience at all. It gave you all the essentials, alongside modern safety technologies such as anti-lock braking to further enhance its nimbleness, while still keeping costs considerably low. It was the perfect first car for any aspiring teenager of the time, and very quickly became known for being a traffic infraction magnet with its 'eccentric' drivers.
description=
description=[b]LX:[/b] The step-up from the base model, this premium trim gave you quite the comfortable ride, improving many facets across the inside and outside of the car that made it quite the desirable option for anyone with a distinguished job. It was the businessman's choice for a somewhat professional, yet exciting experience behind the wheel.
description=
description=[b]LXz:[/b] Ibishu's standard performance option, essentially beefing up the LX model with various performance upgrades to make it quite the perky machine. And unlike many other sports cars, the LXz is one of the few to actually be used to their absolute fullest by its (generally) adrenaline junkie crowd thanks to the relatively low cost of entry and extensive modifiability. As such, it's also a car that has seen such a high rate of being impounded by law enforcement that certain jurisdictions had commandeered certain specimens of it to be used as an effective interceptor, all while looking cool doing it.
description=
description=[b]GTz:[/b] The top-of-the-line performance trim, essentially slapping in a fat turbocharger and some slick five-spoke alloy rims to turn this peppy car into a testament of Japanese automotives. While virtually identical in appearance to the LXz, it's rarely seen on the roads due to its prohibitively high price point and limited numbers made for the US market. It's the definition of a poster car; sick as all hell to whatever child would have this in their bedroom, destined only to live in both greatness and infamy for decades to come. Well, that is if it ever did come.
